The short answer

Four responders almost stopped getting pings after 4.2. It was a step on 12 August, not a seasonal dip.

49→3
pings a week to Vesper, Farlight, The Undertow and Meteor Mite combined (week of 29 Jun → week of 31 Aug)
78→54%
acceptance rate, week before 4.2 → the 4.2 week. Back to 73% by 31 Aug: still below baseline
83
support tickets still open that were filed since 12 Aug (107 filed, to 7 Sep). Before 12 Aug: zero of these two kinds

What the evidence shows

The wait was cut from 90s to 60s and the ranking weights changed the same day. Missed pings went up, and a miss costs a responder's ranking the same as a turn-down.

What it does not show

Which change caused it, or whether responders had less time, never saw the ping, or mis-tapped. I'm saying "not enough information" on the cause, not "speed did it".

Plainly

What I can stand behind, and what I can't

Established (data and code)

  • The 90s→60s wait cut and the weighting change shipped on the same day.
  • Acceptance held 75–78% for six weeks, then 54% in the 4.2 week.
  • The four's missed share rose to 57–75%; the other twelve miss 10–18%.
  • In the code a missed ping lowers ranking exactly as much as a turn-down, and nothing ever raises it back except taking pings.
  • It was not the handlers: the four have four different handlers.

Not established

  • Whether the wait, the weights, or both caused it. They are confounded.
  • Whether responders had less time, never saw the ping, or tapped the wrong button (ticket 3033 shows an accidental decline is possible, with no undo).
  • Anything after 6 Sep.
  • Whether the new weighting was meant to apply to responders who decline (Marcus's 14 Aug question).
  • What responders themselves say: we have never heard from them directly.
Unverified claims are labelled. Priya's seasonal read was an opinion, not a finding.6

In the code, missing a ping is punished like refusing one

Module 4's prompts.md is empty, so there is no prompt of mine to quote here. I won't invent one.
# history.py / config.py

# TODO(2019): should this ease back on its own?

My arithmetic from those numbers

A responder who misses more than 40% of pings loses ranking on average every time (0.08 gained per take, 0.12 lost per miss, no recovery). The four miss 57–75%. This is the mechanism visible in the code, not proven by an experiment.
Finding: 00-rook/code/dispatch-routing (config.py, history.py)11

Module 5 · Super speed

Three options, one choice

OK, let's put together a brief. a production requirements outline. I would prefer three options. As a user from the end use perspective in each scenario of the impacted responder, how should the system behave, the three options are quick fix time saving, end user a targeted approach, and finally long term sustainable approach i have not picked a responder, so my pick would be one fix that address all end user experience

A · Quick fix

A miss stops costing rank; wait back toward 90s. Days of work. Doesn't rescue the four already sunk.

B · Targeted ★ chosen

A, plus recovery, undo, handler view, phone monitoring, away and available. One release if Security and Supply agree.

C · Long-term

Separate "didn't see it" from "doesn't want it", per-responder wait, fairness guard-rail. Two to three releases.

Keeping it from happening again

CheckWhat went wrong around 4.2The question it forces

Success criteriaThe headline metric, acceptance in aggregate, hid four responders losing about 90% of their pings.How will we know it worked, for each person it touches?
